# Planning a prospective study of surgeon gender and surgical care in KwaZulu-Natal

Private case book for authors and reviewers. Do not attach this file to the consultant or supply it to the user actor.

## Public opening

> $causal-consultant It's 18 February 2027. I want to prepare a study of gender differences in surgical care around Durban and Pietermaritzburg. We plan to collect surgical outcomes for patients treated by male and female surgeons, plus possible confounders. How should we design this to reach a causal conclusion?

## Setting and goal

A fictional collaboration of six public-sector hospitals around Durban and Pietermaritzburg, South Africa, preparing prospective collection for adult inpatient general surgery and orthopaedics. Locations are real; institutions, operations, counts and arrangements are fictional.

Planning date: 2027-02-18

Obtain a practical future-study plan specifying which information to collect, when to collect it and which analysis could support a useful, appropriately qualified conclusion about surgical care, within the team's authority and resources.

Expected duration: about 8 to 10 delivered consultant replies. Nominal target: 9. Operational ceiling for a future run: 12. These are private operator metadata, not disclosure triggers or completion criteria.

## Actor's choices

- Prefer a useful question about the care patients receive and changes hospitals could make; a narrower study is acceptable if its connection to the original concern is explained.
- Prefer a short, prioritized collection plan that existing staff could implement over an extensive wish list.
- Do not change access to surgery or assign clinicians for research purposes.
- Do not rank individual surgeons or publish a claim that one gender is inherently better.
- Choose among defensible options after their practical consequences are clear; do not request a particular estimator or a predetermined result.

## Acceptable directions, not a method key

### Prospective observational cohort restricted to services with comparable patients and sufficiently clear clinician or team attribution, with adjustment or standardization based on pre-allocation information.

Can connect a focused collection plan to the original concern while avoiding comparisons driven entirely by specialty or hospital differences.

Claim boundary: Conditional on adequate measured confounding control, overlap, measurement and follow-up. It concerns care under the defined arrangements and population; it does not establish an intrinsic causal effect of gender or guarantee that every service can be compared.

Relevant fact references: f-allocation, f-team-attribution, f-baseline, f-overlap

### Narrow the study to an actionable allocation, communication or support practice after defining what the practice is and how its variation and implementation can be observed.

May produce a more directly useful hospital decision when broad identity-group comparisons lack interpretable allocation or common support.

Claim boundary: This changes the question and may require new measurements or permissions. The current book contains no established causal design for a specific practice, so the consultant must keep this proposal conditional rather than assert it is already identified.

Relevant fact references: f-purpose, f-allocation, f-authority, f-patient-preferences

### Begin with a limited approved feasibility audit and a prospectively specified descriptive or risk-adjusted association study, retaining a conditional causal upgrade only if allocation, overlap and outcome ascertainment prove adequate.

Produces an achievable collection plan and useful evidence about care differences when a credible causal comparison cannot yet be supported.

Claim boundary: Associations and feasibility findings are not causal conclusions. Restricting a population, omitting difficult follow-up or obtaining a statistically adjusted result must not silently convert this route into a causal one.

Relevant fact references: f-overlap, f-followup, f-resources, f-audit

## Review and completion

Judge actual disclosures and their use. Do not credit hidden truth as known, force every alternative, or fail a conditional plan merely because future data do not yet exist.

- Distinguish a comparison of care received under defined surgical arrangements from an inherent or biological effect of gender. Clarify the relevant clinician or team and retain the connection to the user's original concern when proposing a different actionable question.
- Do not infer random assignment from an on-call roster or causal adequacy from a large operation count. A causal route needs a credible account of referral, allocation, takeovers and comparable patients; restrict the target or claim when that account is not yet available.
- Translate the selected question into feasible information priorities and timing: initial allocation and actual care roles, important pre-allocation severity and referral factors, a coherent primary outcome and follow-up, and patient or episode identifiers. Avoid indiscriminately adjusting for postoperative variables. If relevant to the selected outcome, clarify the complication definition and source rather than equating every recorded concern with a confirmed event. Planned procedure detail is not automatically pre-allocation information.
- If follow-up, linkage or cohort selection becomes relevant, account for incomplete outcome ascertainment and the population actually included. Absence from records or successful telephone contacts must not silently define an event-free cohort or replace the proposed population.
- Fit the proposed collection to authority, workload and participation constraints. Treat voluntary identity information, confidentiality and timely clinical care as substantive design issues. Do not assume management support authorizes records access, compel a binary identity response or claim ethics approval is already granted.
- Tie any analysis recommendation to the selected population, care contrast and identified assumptions. Address service and clinician dependence when appropriate, and do not present matching, adjustment or flexible prediction as automatically removing confounding or creating overlap. If experience is measured, explain whether comparing similar experience changes the target; do not automatically adjust for it or for the completed operation and downstream care.
- Use the small answers the actor provides, preserve unanswered matters as unresolved, and reach a useful collection-and-analysis recommendation without demanding the whole book or repeatedly demanding information already answered, explicitly unavailable or deferred without a new decision-relevant reason. A consequential unanswered question may be revisited. Make clear which attainable check would change the route.

- The coordinator can explain the proposed population, care comparison and practical decision, with the main causal limitation stated plainly.
- The recommendation identifies a short set of collection priorities, their timing and a feasible follow-up approach, rather than demanding all possible confounders.
- Analysis is conditional on the actual allocation and comparability evidence, and at least one attainable preparatory check is connected to the decision it could change.
- Unanswered questions remain visible where consequential, without requiring the actor to disclose every fact card or complete a report.
- The actor has enough guidance to prepare the next practical planning step; if it does not, review identifies the specific unresolved issue that prevented a useful endpoint.

## Consequential failure examples

- Promise a causal estimate merely because the cohort has about 12,000 operations or because many confounders can be included.
- Treat on-duty assignment as randomized despite disclosed takeovers and transfers, without investigating allocation.
- Use scheduled consultant gender as a proxy for an intended hands-on-operator or supervisor exposure despite unresolved role attribution, without explicitly selecting and defining a different scheduled-care policy target.
- Infer gender from names, force everyone into two categories or treat self-described gender as an intrinsic biological treatment.
- Adjust for postoperative complications as if they were baseline patient characteristics, without a target-specific justification.
- Count patients without a hospital return or successful telephone contact as alive and complication-free.
- Recommend changing surgeon assignment, delaying needed surgery or disclosing identifiable performance rankings despite the stated authority and confidentiality constraints.
- Demand universal home visits or a long intake assessment after the staffing and budget limits are disclosed.
- Reject all useful study planning because identity itself is not a simple intervention, without considering an actionable related question or explicitly noncausal evidence.
- Replace unanswered facts with invented audit results, known outcome rates, confirmed approval or an assumption that declined or unavailable information is complete.
