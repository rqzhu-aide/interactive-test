# Preparing a comparison of diabetes medicines and depression

Private case book for authors and reviewers. Do not attach this file to the consultant or supply it to the user actor.

## Public opening

> Please use $causal-consultant. It's 15 October 2026. We are planning a study in southwest England comparing depression after patients start dapagliflozin or empagliflozin. Treatment stays with their usual doctors. Before recruitment, what information should we collect, and how could we make a credible causal comparison?

## Setting and goal

A fictional collaboration of general practices and outpatient teams in and around Bristol, Exeter and Plymouth, England, preparing a prospective study before recruitment.

Planning date: 2026-10-15

Prepare an attainable future study: decide whom to recruit, what information to collect before treatment, how to measure depression and follow people, and which analysis can support an appropriately qualified causal conclusion. A practical conditional plan in the conversation is sufficient; no completed estimate or saved report is required.

Expected duration: about 8 to 10 delivered consultant replies. Nominal target: 9. Operational ceiling for a future run: 12. These are private operator metadata, not disclosure triggers or completion criteria.

## Actor's choices

- Prefer a useful study of adults with type 2 diabetes to a larger mixed clinical population if the consultant explains the tradeoff.
- Prefer preserving ordinary treatment choices and access to care; decline any suggestion to delay indicated treatment or insist on continuing an intolerable medicine.
- Can choose a smaller, better measured study or a preliminary feasibility phase if the explanation connects that choice to what can be concluded.
- Prefer a short list of indispensable measurements over an unrestricted wish list. Can revise recruitment forms and follow-up plans before recruitment.
- If offered realistic alternatives, choose according to their explained measurement quality, staff burden and relevance to patient wellbeing, without demanding a particular estimator or a beneficial drug effect.
- Accept a conditional causal analysis plan with explicit remaining assumptions; do not demand that the consultant guarantee a causal answer.

### f-population: Proposed population and indication

Status: planned. Source: Own notes from the study planning meeting..

Known content: The initial proposal concerns adults with type 2 diabetes receiving their first prescription for either medicine. A heart-failure team would also like to include starters without diabetes, but the team has not decided whether that population belongs in the same study. Prior use of the other medicine has not yet been excluded.

Reveal when: A question about who would enter, clinical indication, eligibility, prior use or whether the populations are comparable.

Possible small reply: We initially meant adults with type 2 diabetes getting their first prescription for either medicine. The heart-failure team also wants to include people without diabetes. We have not decided whether to combine them or exclude previous users of the other medicine.

### f-treatment-choice: Who controls treatment

Status: known. Source: Own discussion with the clinical lead about the research team's remit..

Known content: Patients and their usual clinicians choose treatment in ordinary care. Researchers cannot delay indicated treatment, remove an available option or require continued use after intolerance. The research team can change collection and recruitment arrangements, subject to approvals and resources.

Reveal when: A question about randomization, allocation, treatment control, clinician choice or what researchers can change.

Possible small reply: Patients and their usual clinicians choose the medicine. We can change our recruitment and measurements, but we cannot delay needed treatment, remove an available option or require someone to keep taking an intolerable medicine.

### f-comparison-eligibility: Whether either medicine was a realistic option

Status: unknown. Source: Own planning notes and preliminary discussions with participating clinicians; no eligibility audit has been completed..

Known content: No systematic audit has established how many potential participants could reasonably receive either medicine at the same decision. Clinicians report that prior intolerance, kidney function, frailty, other medicines, indication and patient preference influence choice. A brief treatment-decision form could document these considerations prospectively.

Reveal when: A question about why one medicine is chosen, whether both are suitable, overlap, comparable treatment decisions or important baseline confounders.

Possible small reply: We have not counted who could reasonably receive either drug. Clinicians mention prior intolerance, kidney function, frailty, other medicines, the treatment reason and patient preference. They could record the options considered and reasons on a short form.

Known content: Practices hold routine prescriptions, consultations, diagnoses, laboratory results and referrals. Specialist teams hold clinic letters and hospital prescribing records. Coding and older-record transfer vary. The collaboration has neither a linked research dataset nor permission to extract one at planning time.

Reveal when: A question about available files, existing data, routine information, data access or whether records have already been linked.

Possible small reply: Practices have prescriptions, diagnoses, consultations and laboratory records; specialists have clinic letters. Coding and older records vary. We do not yet have permission to extract them or a linked research dataset.

### f-entry-timing: Prescription, initiation and baseline timing

Status: planned. Source: Own notes on proposed recruitment workflow and data access..

Known content: Recruitment alerts would fire when a prescription is issued. Research contact may be up to ten days later. Issue, GP entry, dispensing and actual start can differ; some prescriptions are never collected. Pharmacy dispensing access is not secured. A call about actual initiation is affordable only for a subgroup.

Reveal when: A question about treatment start, time zero, prescription versus dispensing, baseline timing or how promptly research contact occurs.

Possible small reply: The alert would fire when a prescription is issued, but research contact could be ten days later. Dispensing and actual starting can happen later or never. Pharmacy data are not secured; we could ask a subgroup when they actually started.

### f-depression-outcome: What depression means

Status: planned. Source: Own draft application wording and discussions with the clinical lead..

Known content: The draft says new or recurrent depression without an agreed operational definition. Clinical codes, antidepressant prescriptions and symptom questionnaires are possible but different measures. A first local diagnosis may reflect an old episode, and antidepressants may be prescribed for other indications. No outcome data exist.

Reveal when: A question about depression definition, the primary outcome, diagnoses versus symptoms or outcome validation.

Possible small reply: The draft says new or recurrent depression, but we have not defined it. We could use clinical records or symptom questionnaires. A first local code might be an old episode, and antidepressants sometimes have another purpose.

### f-prior-mental-health: Mental health before treatment

Status: known. Source: Own notes from the clinical team's discussion of prior history..

Known content: The team would like to distinguish no known previous episode, a resolved prior episode and active symptoms at entry. Existing records alone cannot reliably distinguish these; transferred history and care outside the system are incomplete. There is no budget for lifetime diagnostic interviews for everyone.

Reveal when: A question about previous depression, active symptoms at entry, recurrence, historical coverage or whether an absent diagnosis means no history.

Possible small reply: We want to separate no known previous depression, a past episode that resolved and current symptoms. Older and outside records are incomplete, so no code is not a reliable negative history. We cannot fund lifetime diagnostic interviews for everyone.

### f-psychiatric-treatment: Current psychiatric treatment and its timing

Status: planned. Source: Own draft baseline medication-reconciliation notes.

Known content: A short baseline reconciliation could ask which mental-health medicines are currently taken, their stated purpose, and the reported dates of recent starts, stops or changes. Approximate dates and unknown timing would remain explicit. Outside prescriptions may need patient confirmation, retaining that source and uncertainty. This information has not been collected or checked for completeness. It would compete for space on the existing short form; no comprehensive outside-record linkage is secured.

Reveal when: Asks about current psychiatric medicines, their purpose, recent changes, or whether treatment dates are known before prescribing.

Possible small reply: We could record current mental-health medicines, their purpose and approximate dates of recent changes, retaining unknown timing. Outside prescriptions may need patient confirmation; we have not checked completeness.

Known content: An unaudited estimate is 1,800 to 2,400 relevant prescribing decisions in a recruitment year across 42 practices and three outpatient teams. These are not unique eligible new users. Proposed funding covers a part-time nurse, an analyst, modest record validation and repeated questionnaires for about 350 participants.

Reveal when: A question about sample size, staffing, budget, sites, feasibility or how much additional measurement can be funded.

Possible small reply: The unaudited forecast is 1,800 to 2,400 relevant prescribing decisions in a recruitment year, not unique eligible new users.

### f-baseline-form: Prospective collection before prescribing

Status: planned. Source: Own preliminary discussions with clinic staff about the proposed collection form..

Known content: Staff say a short form and questionnaire before or at the prescribing consultation are possible, but extensive research interviews at every visit are not. Content can be revised and could include indication, treatment history, options considered, reasons an option is unsuitable, current symptoms, clinical measurements and patient preferences.

Reveal when: A question about which measurements can be added before treatment, staff workflow or feasibility of a baseline form.

Possible small reply: Staff could use a short form before or at prescribing, and we can still change its contents. They cannot manage a long research interview at every consultation. The draft includes treatment reasons, previous treatment, symptoms and the patient's preference.

### f-diabetes-severity: Dated pretreatment diabetes severity

Status: planned. Source: Own draft clinical-measurement specification.

Known content: The collection list could specify diabetes diagnosis date or approximate duration, the latest pretreatment HbA1c with its measurement date, and weight or BMI with its date. Availability and recency in existing records have not been checked. Extra research testing is not funded. A value measured after prescribing is not automatically a pretreatment value.

Reveal when: Asks which diabetes severity measurements could be collected, how current they are, or whether their dates precede treatment.

Possible small reply: We could specify diabetes duration and the latest pretreatment HbA1c and weight, keeping their dates. We have not checked how recent those values usually are, and extra testing is not funded.

### f-treatment-regimen: Current regimen and simultaneous prescribing changes

Status: planned. Source: Own draft treatment-decision form.

Known content: The draft form records previous treatment but does not distinguish medicines still taken from remote exposure, whether the study drug is added or replaces something, or other treatment changes made at that same consultation. A short current-regimen and same-visit changes section could be proposed within the existing form. No such information has yet been collected or audited.

Reveal when: Asks about current medicines, addition versus replacement, or whether the study drug is the only treatment change at the prescribing visit.

Possible small reply: Our draft records previous treatment, but not which medicines are still taken or changed at the same visit. We could add a short section on current medicines and changes at that visit.

### f-richer-baseline: Social circumstances and incomplete confounders

Status: planned. Source: Own draft questionnaire and budget notes..

Known content: Social support, bereavement, financial strain and treatment outside the local system are not reliably present in diagnosis codes. Additional questions about these topics are affordable for a smaller consenting subgroup, creating a choice between broad sparse recruitment and richer measurement in fewer participants.

Reveal when: A question about social circumstances, unrecorded confounders, information outside routine records or the tradeoff between measurement depth and recruitment breadth.

Possible small reply: Routine codes do not reliably cover social support, bereavement, financial strain or treatment elsewhere. We could ask about these in a smaller consenting group. Measuring them carefully for everyone is beyond the current budget.

### f-questionnaire-followup: Repeated symptoms and observation opportunities

Status: planned. Source: Own draft follow-up schedule and patient-representative meeting notes..

Known content: The draft questionnaire schedule is entry and three, six and twelve months for about 350 participants. Sampling and invitation across sites and treatments are undecided. Reveal when: A question about follow-up frequency, symptom questionnaires, equal observation, subgroup recruitment or participation access.

Possible small reply: Questionnaires are proposed at entry and three, six and twelve months. We have not chosen how to invite the 350-person subgroup. Routine contact differs between clinics; patients want paper and telephone options as well as online responses.

Possible small reply: We proposed twelve months. People may stop, restart or switch, and care stays with their clinicians. We have not decided whether our question concerns starting treatment or remaining on it, or how to handle a second eligible prescription decision.

## Acceptable directions, not a method key

### Prospective cohort of clinically comparable new users under ordinary care, with a brief treatment-decision form, supported baseline confounders and a validated routine-record depression outcome.

Uses broader recruitment while improving key information at the treatment decision and checking the meaning and completeness of coded outcomes. Regression, standardization, matching, weighting or doubly robust estimation can be appropriate if justified by eventual data and target.

Claim boundary: An observational comparison of the specified prescription or initiation strategy within a supportable population, conditional on confounding, measurement and follow-up assumptions. Does not establish the effect for all users, all clinical indications or sustained treatment automatically.

Relevant fact references: f-population, f-comparison-eligibility, f-existing-records, f-entry-timing, f-depression-outcome, f-prior-mental-health, f-linkage-followup-gaps

### Smaller prospective cohort with richer baseline mental-health and social information and repeated symptoms measured on a common schedule.

Makes patient-relevant symptom trajectories or a prespecified symptom summary more consistently observable, with a feasible collection burden. An appropriately designed nested subgroup within a broader cohort is also acceptable.

Claim boundary: The outcome is the specified symptom construct, not automatically incident or recurrent clinical depression. Selection, smaller sample size, dropout and residual confounding remain. Generalization beyond the recruited subgroup needs support.

Relevant fact references: f-population, f-comparison-eligibility, f-entry-timing, f-resources, f-prior-mental-health, f-richer-baseline, f-questionnaire-followup, f-distress-response

### Focused feasibility phase before committing to the main comparison, coupled with a conditional main-study plan.

Checks attainable access, eligible overlap, event or symptom information, baseline collection workflow and follow-up capacity before spending the main grant. The consultant should prioritize the few checks most likely to change the proposed design.

Claim boundary: The phase establishes whether and how to launch a credible main study; it is not itself evidence of a treatment effect. Refusal without an actionable feasibility objective does not satisfy this route.

Relevant fact references: f-comparison-eligibility, f-existing-records, f-resources, f-precision-unknown

## Review and completion

Judge actual disclosures and their use. Do not credit hidden truth as known, force every alternative, or fail a conditional plan merely because future data do not yet exist.

- Provide an attainable prospective collection and analysis plan, with a meaningful improvement over merely listing methods or refusing observational inference.
- Align the population, treatment decision and causal target without silently treating all indications or prior treatment histories as interchangeable. When relevant, distinguish the selected drug from simultaneous changes to the care package.
- Make treatment classification, entry and collection timing coherent. Use measurement and treatment-history dates when they matter to the chosen comparison. Collection of severity, current medicines or later changes does not make every variable an adjustment target; preserve unknown timing and distinguish baseline state, treatment components and downstream changes.
- Define a depression outcome whose meaning is supported by the collection plan.
- Match causal claims and precision to the data actually proposed and the access still unresolved.
- Avoid equating missing contact with a negative outcome, and explain how treatment changes and follow-up coverage affect the chosen target.
- If recommending repeated questionnaires, address subgroup selection, observation timing and a feasible response to serious distress.
- Keep the design within the team's treatment authority, participant protection and unresolved confidential-data access.

- The visible conversation gives the coordinator a coherent attainable choice of population, comparison, outcome, priority collection and conditional analysis, with important assumptions and unresolved access or precision limits attached.
- The consultant explains which remaining information could materially change its recommendation and what to arrange before recruitment, rather than asking for an exhaustive data inventory.
- The actor can choose an explained feasible route or accept a bounded feasibility phase under the stated preferences. It may request a short integrated plan once substantive advice is available and no current narrow question needs answering.
- The endpoint is preparation for a future study. No outcome estimate, completed data extraction, saved report, specific method or full disclosure of the information book is required.
- Eight to ten consultant replies is the descriptive expected length, not a turn-indexed disclosure schedule, minimum reply requirement or permission to pad a completed conversation. An operational cap is set separately by the case runner.

## Consequential failure examples

- Promise a causal conclusion because the two drugs are from the same class, the groups can be matched or the sample has thousands of prescriptions.
- Combine first use, switching after intolerance, diabetes and heart-failure-only prescribing without considering their distinct treatment decisions and targets.
- Use a questionnaire completed ten days after treatment as an unqualified pretreatment confounder or require future adherence to enter the cohort.
- Count no local depression code as no previous episode, count every antidepressant prescription as depression, or silently change from clinical depression to symptom scores.
- Drop everyone with no later visit and interpret the remainder as the complete baseline population, or treat absent hospital or mortality linkage as complete event-free follow-up.
- Recommend an instrument based only on prescribing habit or cost preference without establishing its causal conditions.
- Recommend routine linkage or distress screening as immediately launchable despite unresolved data access or an unfunded clinical response route.
- Keep asking for already unavailable power inputs or refuse all planning instead of supplying a useful conditional design or bounded feasibility phase.
- Treat an actor's omitted answer as confirmation, lack of knowledge or proof that a measurement cannot be collected.
- Require every hidden fact or a particular named estimator for success, even when a different coherent route answers the user's planning need.
