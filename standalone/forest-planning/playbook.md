# Planning a rural household-support and forest study in Peru

Private case book for authors and reviewers. Do not attach this file to the consultant or supply it to the user actor.

## Public opening

> $causal-consultant It's 8 February 2027. I want to prepare a study of whether food and school-transport assistance changes forest clearing in rural Peru. Deliveries have not started. What information should we collect, and how could we design the analysis to reach a credible causal conclusion?

## Setting and goal

A fictional prospective evaluation in rural localities near Moyobamba, Rioja and Lamas in San Martin, Peru. The places are real; the program, operational records, quantities and planned events are invented.

Planning date: 2027-02-08

Before booking fieldwork in March, decide what comparison and baseline information to collect, how to allocate a limited survey budget, and what causal question and analysis the planned rollout can realistically support. A useful conditional plan in the conversation is sufficient; no effect estimates or saved report are required.

Expected duration: about 8 to 10 delivered consultant replies. Nominal target: 9. Operational ceiling for a future run: 12. These are private operator metadata, not disclosure triggers or completion criteria.

## Actor's choices

- Prefer an evaluation that does not delay or withhold assistance and does not make support conditional on research participation.
- Want a useful conclusion about the program and forest change, but have not committed to locality offers, participating households or coverage as the target.
- Can accept a narrower population, a shorter common follow-up or a staged study when the consultant explains the practical and scientific consequences.
- Can trade survey length and interviews per locality against coverage of more localities; prefer a small set of consequential measurements over an unaffordable comprehensive survey.
- Can propose limited mapping or delivery-record improvements within existing resources, but cannot promise additional funds or approvals.
- Do not require a favorable forest effect. Accept an explicit limit on causal interpretation when no defensible comparison can be obtained.

### f-package: What the assistance consists of

Status: planned. Source: Own notes of the proposed assistance package.

Known content: Every enrolled household is to receive a monthly staple-food basket through a distribution point. Households with school-age children may also receive school-transport seats where service exists. There are no cash payments. Benefits are not conditional on forest conservation, land ownership, research participation or perfect school attendance. Transport availability makes the package heterogeneous.

Reveal when: A question about what the program provides, how it differs between households or what conditions attach to benefits.

Possible small reply: It is a monthly food basket, with school-transport seats for families with children where that service exists. There is no cash payment or conservation condition. The transport part will differ between places.

### f-delivery-records: Offers, enrollment and receipt can differ

Status: planned. Source: Own notes on feasible delivery-record changes.

Known content: Collection can differ from enrollment because of travel, work, absence, stockouts and sharing food with relatives. Proposed logs can distinguish offers, enrollment, scheduled delivery, collection, basket contents and transport availability, including announcement and notification dates. Staff can add a few fields, but have rejected a second system requiring weekly entry. Route changes, shortages and appeals would use existing records.

Reveal when: A question about measuring exposure, attendance or actual receipt, documenting rollout changes, or what administrative information can be added.

Possible small reply: We can ask for a few fields separating offers, enrollment and actual collection, with dates, basket contents and transport availability. Staff will not maintain a second weekly reporting system. Enrollment does not guarantee that a household collects its basket.

Known content: The GIS analyst has public annual forest-loss layers for 2017 through 2024, settlement coordinates, broad land-cover maps and roads. ### f-forest-measurement: Forest loss is not necessarily conversion

Status: unknown. Source: Own notes of unresolved forest-measurement questions.

Known content: The team has not established how often mapped tree-cover loss represents natural forest converted to another use rather than tree-crop rotation, fire or storm damage. Cloud, small clearings and gradual degradation can obscure changes. Limited participatory mapping and a sample of consented plot visits are possible proposals, but their extent and budget allocation are not fixed. A complete cadastral survey is unaffordable. No pre-program natural-forest mask, classification rule or baseline reference date has been chosen. The broad land-cover files have not been checked for distinguishing plantations from natural forest. The land contributing to the outcome remains a planning choice.

Reveal when: A question about what forest loss means, measurement error, validating satellite outcomes or affordable ground checks. Also asks which land counts as forest at baseline, the reference date, or how plantations would be distinguished.

Possible small reply: We do not yet know how often the mapped losses are permanent forest conversion rather than crop rotation, fire or storm damage. Limited mapping and consented plot visits might help, but we cannot afford a complete land survey.

### f-outcome-geography: Where the forest outcome belongs

Status: known. Source: Own notes of the mapping discussion.

Known content: Settlement-centered circles need not match resource-use areas. Some households farm dispersed or rented plots, and some use collectively managed land. Neighboring settlements may share forest. Existing boundary files overlap or leave gaps; verified household farm boundaries do not exist. A stable geographic outcome area still needs definition before later changes are examined.

Reveal when: A question about linking forest change to households or localities, plot boundaries, buffers or the geographical outcome unit.

Possible small reply: We do not have verified household farm boundaries. Families may use scattered, rented or shared land, and locality boundaries overlap or have gaps. A circle around a settlement would not necessarily describe the forest its households use.

## Acceptable directions, not a method key

### A prospective locality-level comparison of earlier and later rollout cohorts, with a common feasible follow-up horizon and a checked pre-program forest history.

Uses planned implementation variation without withholding assistance; directs collection toward entry decisions, comparable outcome histories, geography and concurrent changes.

Claim boundary: A causal interpretation still requires a credible untreated counterfactual and appropriate assumptions about trends, anticipation and interference. Priority changes, hardship and roads may defeat those assumptions. Household receipt effects and a universal two-year effect do not follow automatically.

Relevant fact references: f-rollout, f-ordering, f-authority, f-calendar, f-outcome-geography, f-forest-archives, f-routes

### A matched or weighted prospective comparison of program localities with suitable external areas, considered only after access, outcome comparability and reasons for exclusion from the plan are established.

Could preserve an untreated comparison when later program localities no longer supply one, while making required access and comparability checks explicit.

Claim boundary: The twelve neighboring areas are merely potential participants with different road access. Matching measured characteristics or a favorable pre-period fit does not establish exchangeability or remove time-varying confounding.

Relevant fact references: f-authority, f-ordering, f-forest-archives, f-outcome-geography, f-concurrent-changes

### A prospective household component for hardship or behavior, paired with an explicitly separate locality forest analysis or an explained narrower target.

Uses the feasible survey to understand household outcomes and program reach, while distinguishing individual measurements from geographical forest change.

Claim boundary: Causal household comparisons require defensible exposure, timing, support and confounding assumptions. Mechanisms are not identified merely by observing post-program household behavior, and locality forest changes cannot be attributed to particular households without suitable linkage.

Relevant fact references: f-target, f-register, f-package, f-delivery-records, f-baseline-collection, f-followup, f-survey-budget

### A staged implementation and measurement study, followed by a conditional causal evaluation if comparison and measurement requirements can be met.

Provides useful delivery, recruitment, mapping and outcome-quality information before making causal claims that the available rollout cannot yet support.

Claim boundary: The implementation brief and descriptive forest changes are not estimates of a causal program effect. State which decisions or evidence are still needed and do not quietly replace the original causal goal.

Relevant fact references: f-target, f-calendar, f-outcome-geography, f-forest-measurement, f-survey-budget

## Review and completion

Judge actual disclosures and their use. Do not credit hidden truth as known, force every alternative, or fail a conditional plan merely because future data do not yet exist.

- Help choose an attainable target, distinguishing locality program availability from household receipt and recognizing that food plus variable transport is the actual package. Do not silently redefine the intervention as cash or treat all participants as receiving the same components. If coverage is selected, define its denominator and timing; an intensity question is not interchangeable with an offer effect.
- Investigate why entry differs and what researchers control. The plan must respect hardship priorities and must not declare rollout randomized, create a fictional cutoff or promise causal identification from adjustment alone.
- Match the forest outcome and aggregation to the chosen causal question. Stable geographic definitions and measurement validation matter; settlement buffers do not establish household attribution, and tree-cover loss does not necessarily mean conversion of natural forest. Define the baseline forest domain when the chosen outcome depends on it, without conditioning inclusion on later forest change.
- Prioritize information that can change identification, interpretation or practical feasibility within the budget. Address baseline timing, selection into survey lists and distinction between offers and receipts when they matter. Avoid exhaustive unaffordable variable menus.
- Align the causal follow-up horizon with staggered entry and funding. The September 2028 implementation brief cannot report two-year outcomes for all planned cohorts, and January 2029 entrants cannot provide two years of follow-up by December 2029.
- If comparing entry cohorts, explain the untreated comparison and trend assumptions, anticipation, contextual changes, shared-route dependence and spillovers. Avoid using already treated units as clean untreated comparisons or treating 84 localities as 84 independent rollout decisions without investigating routes. When eligibility-rule changes matter to the chosen comparison, retain the unresolved status and explain what version history would need checking.
- If proposing a household-level causal comparison, address incomplete registers, selective collection, feasible pre-exposure confounding measurements, missing follow-up and lack of verified household-linked forest outcomes. Do not solve selection by conditioning only on future collectors or imply that matching creates ignorability.
- Keep assistance independent of research participation and make proposed interviews, linkage and mapping conditional on appropriate consent, community arrangements, access and confidentiality protections. Treat permissions as unresolved operational requirements, without claiming the assistant has approved the study.
- Reach a useful collection and analysis recommendation without invented outcomes or a mandatory saved report. A conditional causal route, staged feasibility work or a justified limited descriptive study can satisfy the goal when its residual limits and next decisions are clear.

- The coordinator understands a defensible primary target or a clearly conditional choice between a small number of targets.
- The advice identifies priority pre-delivery measurements and comparison information, and relates the household sampling and mapping tradeoffs to the available budget.
- The proposed analysis respects selection into rollout, the chosen outcome geography, feasible follow-up and the permissions needed for its data collection.
- Residual identification limits and the next practical decision are explicit; useful conditional advice does not require every fact card to have been disclosed.
- A justified staged or descriptive plan is acceptable if the consultant explains why the original causal claim remains unresolved and what could make a later causal analysis credible.

## Consequential failure examples

- Recommend delaying food deliveries or changing hardship priorities to manufacture controls despite the stated authority limit.
- Describe administrative rollout as randomized, invent a numerical eligibility threshold or use delivery readiness as an instrument without a credible exclusion argument.
- Claim that an observed forest decline proves that the assistance prevented deforestation, before any intervention outcomes exist or without a defensible comparison.
- Treat satellite tree-cover loss, alert counts and permanent natural-forest conversion as interchangeable outcomes without checking definitions.
- Use settlement buffers as verified household farms or count overlapping forest areas independently without discussing the consequences.
- Promise two post-start years for January 2029 entrants using monitoring that ends in December 2029.
- Assume 720 households or 84 localities establishes adequate power, while ignoring linked rollout routes and uncertainty in event rates or outcome variance.
- Condition the main analysis on collecting benefits or completing all interviews without explaining the resulting selection and target change.
- Ask for an unaffordable complete cadastral survey, or assume pending road records, research approval and land-entry permission have already been secured.
- Treat leaders' permission as individual consent, link sensitive identifiers without an appropriate agreement, or propose public maps accusing identifiable families of illegal clearing.
- Penalize the consultant for failing to recite an undisclosed fact instead of examining whether it sought consequential attainable information and qualified its advice.
- Reward an actor that answers several independent questions, dumps the hidden book or invents facts to reach the expected conversation length.
