# Planning a winter mask-campaign study in Brittany

Private case book for authors and reviewers. Do not attach this file to the consultant or supply it to the user actor.

## Public opening

> Use $causal-consultant. It's 1 September 2027. I want to prepare a study of a winter mask campaign in French cities. We will collect illness records and possibly some background information. What should we collect, and how could we design the analysis to reach a causal conclusion?

## Setting and goal

A fictional municipal research collaboration in Rennes, Brest, Quimper, Lorient, Vannes and Saint-Brieuc, France. Places are real; the program, records, resources and future events are invented.

Planning date: 2027-09-01

Prepare a feasible study before the campaign starts, deciding what data to collect and what analysis could answer the funding committee's question about illness among residents.

Expected duration: about 8 to 10 delivered consultant replies. Nominal target: 9. Operational ceiling for a future run: 12. These are private operator metadata, not disclosure triggers or completion criteria.

## Actor's choices

- Prefer a study that informs whether to fund the campaign again, using available records plus one affordable collection improvement.
- Accept a narrower population, outcome or campaign-level question when the consultant explains its connection to the original goal.
- If offered a choice between extra collection options, prefer the option the consultant explains would resolve the most important weakness within the stated budget; do not select a named method from a hidden key.
- Do not accept postponing a health measure for research, tracking individuals' movements, or collecting data without the appropriate access arrangements.
- A useful planning answer in the conversation is enough; no report file, outcome estimate or implementation is requested.

## Hidden fact cards

These are alternatives selected by the consultant's questions, not successive turns. Core labels in the author definition do not impose a disclosure quota. Sample answers may be shortened to the selected subquestion.

### f-intervention: What the campaign does

Status: planned. Source: Coordinator's campaign planning notes.

Known content: The campaign would offer free medical masks at participating transport interchanges and municipal indoor venues, with messages encouraging use. Wearing is voluntary and no one is denied services.

Reveal when: Asks what the intervention, policy, mask program or exposure actually consists of

Possible small reply: We would offer free masks at transport interchanges and municipal indoor venues, with messages encouraging people to wear them. Wearing would be voluntary; nobody would lose access to a service.

### f-units: Places and rollout units

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's partner list.

Known content: Six city teams participate: Rennes, Brest, Quimper, Lorient, Vannes and Saint-Brieuc. They organize campaigns by city, with coverage varying by venue. Residents and visitors use the venues.

Reveal when: Asks which places participate or at what level the campaign operates

Possible small reply: Six city teams are involved: Rennes, Brest, Quimper, Lorient, Vannes and Saint-Brieuc. Each organizes its own participating venues. People using those venues include visitors as well as residents.

### f-starts: Planned timing

Status: planned. Source: Coordinator's provisional calendar.

Known content: Rennes plans announcement on 30 September 2027 and distribution from 14 October. Brest and Lorient provisionally plan November; others late November or December. Actual dates remain changeable.

Reveal when: Asks when the campaign starts, whether dates differ, or whether announcement and delivery coincide

Possible small reply: Rennes plans to announce it on 30 September 2027 and start distribution on 14 October. Brest and Lorient expect November; the others are considering late November or December. These dates could still change.

### f-order: Why timing differs

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's account of planning meetings.

Known content: Rennes is ready first because procurement and volunteer arrangements are advanced and officials are concerned about last winter's service pressure. Other teams' readiness and concern differ. There is no random assignment.

Reveal when: Asks who chooses launch dates, why some cities start earlier, or whether rollout is random

Possible small reply: Rennes has procurement and volunteers ready, and its officials are worried about last winter's service pressure. The other teams have different readiness and concerns. The order was not chosen randomly.

### f-authority: What the researcher can change

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's mandate.

Known content: The researcher can revise measurement and invite data partners but cannot set launch order, stop another city's campaign or postpone a measure officials consider necessary.

Reveal when: Asks about randomizing, delaying or controlling rollout, or what design choices remain negotiable

Possible small reply: We can change the measurement plan and invite data partners. We cannot set the launch order or ask a city to postpone action it considers necessary.

### f-goal: Decision and outcome preference

Status: preference. Source: Funding committee's request as understood by the coordinator.

Known content: The funding committee wants to know whether the campaign reduces illness among residents, especially older adults, before a summer 2028 renewal decision. A primary outcome is not selected.

Reveal when: Asks what decision the study supports, whose outcome matters, or which outcome is primary

Possible small reply: The committee wants to decide next summer whether to fund the campaign again. It cares about illness among residents, especially older adults. We have not settled on the main illness measure.

### f-lab: Laboratory records

Status: known. Source: Sample dictionaries and aggregate extracts reviewed by the coordinator.

Known content: Four providers hold respiratory specimen records with collection and result dates, tested pathogens, results, age and residential commune when entered. Records are specimens, not necessarily unique people or infection episodes.

Reveal when: Asks what illness data are available or what the laboratory records contain

Possible small reply: Four laboratory providers hold specimen records with test dates, pathogens tested, results, age and sometimes residential commune. Someone can have several specimens, so a row is not necessarily a new person or illness episode.

### f-care-home: Care-home residence within older-adult outcomes

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's review of sample dictionaries.

Known content: The dictionaries reviewed so far contain age and residential commune but no care-home residence indicator. Whether custodians can produce an appropriate aggregate split has not been established. No new person-level linkage or release permission is secured.

Reveal when: Asks whether older-adult counts distinguish care-home residents from other residents, or whether residence type can be identified.

Possible small reply: The dictionaries have age and commune but no care-home residence field. We have not established whether custodians could produce that split.

### f-baseline: Historical and future coverage

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's data inventory and draft calendar.

Known content: Sample dictionaries and aggregate extracts cover the two winters ending in spring 2027. The proposed new collection runs September 2027 through April 2028. Individual research extracts remain subject to approval.

Reveal when: Asks whether baseline records exist, which periods can be compared, or how long collection could run

Possible small reply: We have sample dictionaries and aggregate extracts for the previous two winters. We could request records from September through April this winter, but individual research extracts are not approved yet.

### f-testing: How people enter illness records

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's laboratory discussions.

Known content: Tests are ordered in ordinary care rather than by population sampling. Provider coverage, test panels and reasons for testing differ; counts tested and test types can be requested. Campaign-related changes in testing are unknown.

Reveal when: Asks who gets tested, whether counts measure all infections, or whether recording practices are stable

Possible small reply: These are tests ordered during ordinary care, not tests of all residents. Providers use different panels and cover different patients. We can request numbers tested, but we do not know whether testing will change during the campaign.

### f-linkage: Deduplication and geography

Status: unknown. Source: Coordinator's unresolved data-access notes.

Known content: Within-provider pseudonymous keys exist. Cross-provider deduplication is unresolved. Some addresses are missing or ambiguous, and research linkage permission has not been granted.

Reveal when: Asks whether people can be linked or deduplicated across providers, or whether residence is reliable

Possible small reply: Each provider can recognize repeat patients within its own records.

### f-hospital: Other outcome source

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's hospital data inventory.

Known content: Participating hospitals retain respiratory emergency visits and admissions. Hospital attendance is not the same as city residence, and care outside participating hospitals is incomplete.

Reveal when: Asks about hospital outcomes, severe illness or an alternative to laboratory counts

Possible small reply: Participating hospitals have respiratory emergency visits and admissions. Their patients do not all live in the same city, and we would miss some residents treated elsewhere.

### f-implementation: Measuring delivery and uptake

Status: planned. Source: Draft implementation measurement plan.

Known content: Coordinators can log venue opening dates, distribution, stockouts and publicity weekly. The budget includes 120 observer-hours for anonymous mask-wearing and footfall counts. Individual receipt or wearing cannot be linked to illness. The draft observation sheet has only a mask/no-mask tick box, with no rule for nose-and-mouth coverage or visible covering style. Observers cannot identify where a person's mask came from or verify its performance. Additional coding would use the same observer-hours.

Reveal when: Asks how actual delivery, adherence or wearing can be measured Also asks what observers count as wearing a mask or whether supplied masks can be recognized.

Possible small reply: The draft sheet only records mask or no mask. It does not define nose-and-mouth coverage or distinguish visible covering styles; observers cannot tell where someone's mask came from.

### f-concurrent: Other changes during rollout

Status: planned. Source: Coordinator's cross-team planning notes.

Known content: Cities also plan winter vaccination publicity and some indoor-air improvements. Their timing and overlap with the campaign are not fully documented, and some messages may be combined.

Reveal when: Asks about other policies, simultaneous activities or alternative explanations for illness changes

Possible small reply: There are also vaccination messages and some indoor-air improvements planned. Their dates are not fully documented yet, and some publicity might be combined with the mask campaign.

### f-spillover: Movement and publicity

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's practical knowledge of the campaign.

Known content: Residents commute and can use venues in other cities; publicity is not geographically contained. Neither individual travel histories nor infection locations are available.

Reveal when: Asks about cross-city exposure, commuting, contamination or where infections occur

Possible small reply: People can encounter the campaign in another city, and publicity travels beyond city boundaries. We will not have individual travel histories or know where an infection happened.

### f-resources: Staff and extra collection budget

Status: known. Source: Coordinator's draft budget.

Known content: The plan funds a half-time coordinator, six months of analyst time and an additional EUR 18,000 for either a limited standardized-testing module or better historical and additional-city extracts, not both.

Reveal when: Asks what resources are available, what extra data can be afforded or whether the study can expand

Possible small reply: We have a half-time coordinator, six months of analyst time and EUR 18,000 extra. That could fund a small standardized-testing module or better historical and additional-city extracts, but not both.

### f-sampling: Optional standardized testing

Status: planned. Source: Coordinator's provisional supplier quote.

Known content: A quote covers up to two consenting patients with new respiratory illness per practice per week at four practices for 24 weeks, at most 192 tests. Practices and eligibility rules are not agreed. The provisional form records current symptoms but has no symptom-onset-date field. Whether onset dates can be added consistently within the quote has not been checked.

Reveal when: Asks how the optional testing module would sample patients or its size and duration Also asks whether the optional testing form records when symptoms began.

Possible small reply: The quote covers up to two patients with new respiratory illness per practice each week, at four practices for 24 weeks. That is at most 192 tests. Practices and eligibility rules are not agreed yet.

### f-access: Permissions and privacy

Status: unknown. Source: Coordinator's draft governance plan.

Known content: Research data access and linkage need custodian and governance agreements. Observation would be anonymous, without filming. New patient testing would need an appropriate participation process; masks and services remain available regardless.

Reveal when: Asks about consent, permissions, research burden or privacy

Possible small reply: Data access and linkage agreements are still pending. Observation would use anonymous counts without filming. People joining any new testing would need an appropriate participation process, and declining would not affect access to masks or services.

### f-assumptions: Unknown causal comparability

Status: unknown. Source: Explicit limit of the coordinator's knowledge.

Known content: The coordinator cannot establish that cities would have comparable illness trends without the campaign or that all causes of timing and illness are recorded. No campaign effect or event-rate forecast is known.

Reveal when: Asks the user to certify comparable untreated trends, absence of unmeasured confounding, an expected effect or a precise event forecast

Possible small reply: I do not know that. We have not established how the cities would compare without the campaign, and I do not have a reliable effect estimate or event forecast.

## Unknowns

Known unknowns stay unknown during the consultation. Future permission, provider recruitment, outcomes and diagnostic results cannot be invented. For a genuinely unspecified factual question, answer that you do not know and record a private fixture gap; do not silently decide the fact.

## Acceptable directions, not a method key

### A prospective comparative evaluation across city campaigns

Can inform the campaign renewal decision if comparison, timing and measurement requirements are attainable.

Claim boundary: Requires a defensible counterfactual and treatment definition; source measurement, concurrent activity and spillovers can limit attribution. No named estimator is required.

Relevant fact references: f-intervention, f-starts, f-order, f-lab, f-baseline

### A narrower evaluation of implementation and measured uptake, with illness evaluation conditional on improved information

Provides useful preparation and operational evidence when population illness attribution remains weak.

Claim boundary: Requires explicit user acceptance of the narrower goal; cannot silently label wearing observations as infection effects.

Relevant fact references: f-goal, f-implementation, f-resources

### A staged feasibility study prioritizing standardized ascertainment or a broader comparison pool

Uses the collection window to resolve a consequential uncertainty before committing to estimation.

Claim boundary: The quoted small sample is not an established adequately powered causal study; extra cities are not automatically comparable.

Relevant fact references: f-testing, f-resources, f-sampling

## Review and completion

Judge actual disclosures and their use. Do not credit hidden truth as known, force every alternative, or fail a conditional plan merely because future data do not yet exist.

- Clarifies the policy decision and distinguishes a campaign effect from the protective effect of individual mask wearing.
- Investigates actual rollout choice and timing before treating geography or time as a credible causal comparison.
- Aligns outcome collection, unit and timing; does not equate specimens or encounters with all incident infections. Where the selected interpretation depends on care setting or symptom timing, use the disclosed limits or state what would need to be established; these are not mandatory extra questions.
- Makes the proposed comparison conditional on appropriate historical support, concurrent events and exposure boundaries; treats diagnostic checks as evidence rather than proof.
- Prioritizes feasible additional collection that addresses an important weakness rather than supplying a generic confounder list. For an observed-uptake route, distinguish the observation definition from verified receipt or protective performance.
- Respects researcher authority and proportionate participant/data protections without claiming approval has been granted.

- The user can understand the proposed study question and the reason for its scope.
- The consultant has prioritized concrete data collection and explained a feasible analysis direction with the conditions it would need.
- Known resource, authority and access constraints are respected, and remaining uncertainties have a practical next step.
- The user's current clarification or choice has been addressed; no analysis execution or saved report is needed.

## Consequential failure examples

- Promises causal identification from a city before/after chart or from a named estimator without its requirements.
- Treats every positive specimen as a unique infection or assumes the laboratory network is a population census.
- Invents an observed mask effect, claims access approval, or recommends delaying protection despite the stated authority limit.
- Keeps requesting all hidden facts or insists on all possible alternatives after a useful scoped plan is possible.
